## Method, assumptions and limitations

The research was run on 13 October 2026 as a multi-pass system: one cost-and-regulation pass (Agent 5), seven category passes covering all 20 categories (Agents 1/3/4 combined), one problem-first pass (Agent 2) that started from Israeli consumer complaints rather than products, a trend sweep (Agent 1), five sourcing passes that attached Chinese B2B listings and Israeli retailer prices to every surviving candidate, a gap-fill pass, a demand-validation pass for ranks 19–50 and a skeptical review (Agent 7) of the provisional top 18 before final ranking. Each pass wrote structured JSON; a Python model merged, de-duplicated (20 near-identical listings were merged into their canonical product), priced, scored and ranked the candidates, and this site and the spreadsheet were generated from that data.
How the web was browsed. This environment allows live web search but blocks direct page loads of Alibaba, Made-in-China, Zap, KSP and most other sites. Every URL in this report was returned by a live search on the research date and every "search-snippet" figure (price, MOQ, review count, rank) was read from that search result's text. Pages were not opened, so figures marked search-snippet are observed but not page-verified; figures marked "estimated" are researcher estimates. Supplier listings and prices must be re-quoted before any order.
Unit of comparison. All costs are VAT-exclusive; import VAT (18%) is treated as recoverable working capital. Recommended retail prices include VAT and are anchored on the observed Israeli mid-market (cheapest cross-border listing and premium brand outlier trimmed), with an importer floor of 3× landed cost and a cap of 4.5× (6× for very cheap goods); when Israeli retail does not support 3× landed, the price is capped at 1.5× the local mid-market and the resulting margin is shown as it is. Contribution is computed per order and divided by the assumed units per order (1 for products over ₪120, 1.5 for ₪70–120, 2.2 under ₪70) because cheap items are bought as multipacks or bundles against Israel's typical ₪199 free-shipping threshold.

- Search-only browsing. Product pages on Alibaba, Made-in-China, Global Sources, Zap, KSP and most Israeli retailers could not be opened. Prices, MOQs and review counts come from search-result text on 2026-10-13 and may be stale, promotional or mis-paired to the exact variant. Several Israeli price rows are flagged "URL pairing uncertain" where a category page listed several prices.

- Coverage of Israeli retailers is uneven. Home Center, Bug, Payngo, Shein IL, ACE, Max Stock, Decathlon, Shilav and Lametayel surfaced well; KSP, Ivory and Zap item pages rarely expose prices in search snippets, so some "no Israeli competitor found" results are search blind spots, not true white space. Re-check Zap/KSP manually before relying on a gap.

- Supplier prices are listing prices, not quotes. Global Sources and Made-in-China snippets often show the lowest tier of a range at the highest MOQ. Expect +10–30% for small first orders, plus sample and courier costs. "Verified supplier" badges were only recorded where the snippet showed them.

- Demand signals are mostly US. Amazon sales-tracker estimates (AsInsight and similar) and review counts are US figures; they prove the product category sells, not that Israelis will buy it at the modelled price. Israeli demand was validated with Hebrew retail presence, local press and forum evidence where found.

- Customs duty lines were not confirmed on the official tariff. The shaarolami tariff database could not be opened; rates used (0% electronics/toys, ~12% textiles and plastics, 6–8% blended elsewhere) are from secondary sources and must be checked per HS line with a customs broker.

- Standards Institute costs are planning figures. SII publishes no price list; ₪3k–15k per model for electrical/charger/toy approval and ~₪0–500 for declaration-only goods are estimates; the 2024–25 test-certificate waiver's current status is unconfirmed.

- Advertising economics are the biggest unknown. No Israeli CPM/CPC benchmarks in shekels were found; the base case uses 25% of net price (minimum ₪35) per order, cross-checked against a vendor dataset showing a median Israeli e-commerce cost-per-purchase around $35. Real CAC varies 3× between products and creatives; this is why the 30-day plan tests ads before bulk orders.

- Success probability. No calibrated base-rate model for Israeli import tests exists in this research, so no product carries a numeric probability. The opportunity score ranks relative attractiveness and must not be read as a probability.

- Exchange rate. USD/ILS ≈3.05 was observed in early October 2026 from a currency-aggregator snapshot, not the Bank of Israel; the pessimistic scenario should be re-run at 3.3–3.5 if the shekel weakens.

- Time-boxed research. Shared search budgets limited each pass; 33 otherwise interesting candidates are listed as "not viable at observed Israeli prices" and a further set was not sourced at all. They are listed on this page as evidence of failures, not hidden.
