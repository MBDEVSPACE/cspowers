Skeptical review (Agent 7)
Demand evidence is a mirror snapshot of a US listing (2,426 reviews) and a Slickdeals thread whose own commenter says 'quality is a gamble'; the complaint cluster is specific and warranty-relevant: included tree straps stretching or failing under rated load (one at 92 kg on a 110 kg rating), nets tearing on the first nights, weak zippers, strap stitching parting. Supplier price $5.75 is for a hammock without net: B2B shows $3.95-3.99 (MOQ 500) and $5.00-6.50 (MOQ 500) for parachute hammocks with straps, but net versions list at $6.50-7 (MOQ 16 sets), $6-18 FOB (MOQ 500, Ningbo Eto) and $20.39 (MOQ 20), so a net+straps unit is ~$7.5. Israeli pricing: Home Center parachute hammock with accessories 69.90 (89.90 in store), Decathlon single hammock 79, a separate 4Monster hammock net on Zap ~89, and Zap lists a built-in-net camping hammock without a clear price; the file's 169 comp is the only net version. 139 ILS is therefore plausible but unproven, and Israeli demand for a mosquito-net hammock (vs plain hammock) is niche and seasonal.
Risk flags: supplier price is for no-net hammock; net+straps ~$7.5 FOB; straps/net/zipper failure complaints -> safety and returns; niche in Israel vs plain hammock at 70-80 ILS; seasonal camping demand
