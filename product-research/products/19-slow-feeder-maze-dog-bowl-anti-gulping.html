1 · Product overview

Problem solved
Dogs eat too fast -> vomiting, bloat risk; owners want to slow meals.
Who experiences it / frequency
Owners of labs, beagles, rescues; also cats. — Daily use; replaced yearly; multi-dog homes buy several.
Current solutions & gaps
Hand-feeding, muffin tins. Market leader Outward Hound; gaps: hard to clean, slides on floor, too shallow for big breeds.
Target audience
Dog owners 25-50; vets recommend.
Why now
Huge proven demand (135K reviews) but category dominated by one brand and widely available in IL; win via enrichment bundle and silicone variants.

6 · Regulatory, IP and risk

Israeli standard (SII) likely required
no Pet food contact - request food-grade material declaration; no mandatory SII.
Flags
food contact ·
IP / design-right risk
medium Outward Hound maze designs are branded/possibly design-registered; use generic patterns. [QA review set level: medium]
Return / quality risk class
medium (return reserve 5%)
Skeptical review (Agent 7)
Demand is durable (Outward Hound 134K+ reviews) but the Israeli market is already full at low prices: Zap lists a 500 ml maze bowl at 49 ILS, stainless slow bowls 59-79, Outward Hound Fun Feeder Mini 89 / Large 145, and ACE a foldable slow bowl at 50 ILS. 109 ILS for a generic plastic/silicone maze bowl is above the branded leader's Mini; 59-69 is realistic. Supplier estimate $2.6 is fine (plastic $0.65-0.89 MOQ 1,000; silicone maze $2.20-2.70 MOQ 80; $3.90-4.30 MOQ 100). IP is denser than 'medium' suggests: Towerstar Pets holds a family of US design patents on slow-feed bowls (D1020122, D915686, D895216, D904695) with the Fun Feeder cited as prior art - US patents do not bite in Israel, but any Amazon/EU resale would; pick a distinct, non-spiral/non-maze-identical pattern. Food contact: insist on LFGB/FDA material declaration.
Risk flags: Israeli comps 49-89 ILS; design patents on maze bowls (US); pet food-contact declaration; low unit margin
