1 · Product overview

Problem solved
Sleeping in noisy, lit tents/bases during reserve duty
Who experiences it / frequency
Reservists; spouses/parents buying gifts — Per call-up (frequent since 2023)
Current solutions & gaps
Tactical shops sell items individually; no affordable branded comfort bundle
Target audience
Reservists 22-45, gift buyers
Why now
Hundreds of thousands of reservists have served repeated rotations since 2023; Israeli gear blogs (pakal-il, miluimnikim, timetoedc) list exactly these items, and no retailer sells a packaged 'ערכת מילואים'. Generic travel kits sell at ₪29–50 on Zap, so the kit must earn a premium through framing, quality and giftability.

Skeptical review (Agent 7)
The modelled $2.79 landed-kit cost is not credible for four items plus a printed pouch: realistic component costs (3D mask ~$0.3-0.5, earplugs ~$0.1, inflatable pillow ~$1-1.5, microfiber towel ~$1-1.5, pouch + Hebrew print ~$0.5-0.8) put it at $3.5-5. Israeli pricing: Zap lists generic travel kits (inflatable neck pillow + eye cover + earplugs) at ~29-50 ILS and others at 94-129 ILS incl. shipping; a 139 ILS kit must justify a 3x premium purely on the miluim framing. No 'ערכת מילואים' product was found at any Israeli retailer: that is both the opportunity and the warning (no proven demand; evidence is three blog posts with no sales data). Seasonality is call-up driven and unpredictable. Earplugs sold for sleep avoid medical-device status, but do not claim hearing protection ratings. Avoid IDF/MoD insignia (protected marks).
Risk flags: supplier cost understated (bundle); demand evidence is blogs only; generic travel kits at 29-50 ILS; call-up driven seasonality; IDF insignia IP
Model overrides applied
Thin, non-quantified demand and an understated cost basis; viable only as a 89-99 ILS gift kit sold via Hebrew content marketing after a 100-unit test.
Import & regulatory risk summary
Low: textiles/plastics, no standard; Hebrew labelling; no medical claims on earplugs; duty modelled 6% (mixed HS). Avoid IDF insignia.
