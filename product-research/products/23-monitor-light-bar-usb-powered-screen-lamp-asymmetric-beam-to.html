6 · Regulatory, IP and risk

Israeli standard (SII) likely required
possible USB-A/USB-C 5V powered, no battery, no mains plug (user supplies USB adapter) - keeps SII burden low; CE/RoHS/EMC; LED lighting standards possible; do not bundle a mains adapter
Flags
none
IP / design-right risk
medium BenQ holds design patents on ScreenBar asymmetric optics/clamp in some jurisdictions; use generic OEM designs and avoid 'ScreenBar' name [QA review set level: medium]
Return / quality risk class
medium (return reserve 5%)
Skeptical review (Agent 7)
Demand is durable (category exists since BenQ 2017; Quntis ~3K/month on the US tracker) but Israel is already served by branded mid-tier product: Zap lists Yeelight Pura Lite 179 ILS, Yeelight Pura Pro 319-329, Baseus magnetic bar 215-249, Yeelight Screen Light Bar Pro 410-427, and Bug sells the Xiaomi magnetic reading light bar at 99 ILS. The 789 ILS comp is BenQ ScreenBar, which does not set the price for an unbranded OEM bar; 339 ILS would sit above Baseus and Yeelight with no brand or warranty story. Supplier price $12.5 is realistic: Global Sources shows $7.2-9 (MOQ 1) and an explicitly asymmetric model at $16.8-18.5 (MOQ 1-499). IP: BenQ's product pages state the counterweight clamp is patented; no BenQ-vs-Quntis litigation surfaced, so a generic OEM clamp is a medium, manageable risk if the 'ScreenBar' name and the counterweight clamp geometry are avoided. Regulatory: USB 5V with no bundled adapter is usually released on a 'standard not applicable' basis, but LED luminaire standards are a possibility; do not bundle a mains adapter. Typical complaints: flicker when dimming, clamp fit on thin bezels/curved monitors, uneven light.
Risk flags: branded competition at 179-329 ILS; BenQ patented clamp - avoid copying; PWM flicker complaints; clamp fit on thin/curved monitors; possible LED luminaire standard query at customs
