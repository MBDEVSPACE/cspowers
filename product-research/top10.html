      Skeptical reviewer

      Demand evidence is stronger than the file suggests for Israel specifically: Pointer (2023) and a 2026 bhol report say the leading Israeli theft method is key-cloning through the OBD socket, often with a cellular jammer, and Lahav 433 reported a ~25% spike in attempts in early 2026 after two years of decline. But forum users (4Runner, Lexus UK) argue a port lock does not stop CAN-injection and can be bypassed by probing the CAN wires behind the lock, and it blocks garage diagnostics; fit is vehicle-specific (port position/orientation differs by model). Supplier price is plausible: Alibaba shows an aluminium OBD port lock at $6.25-7 (MOQ 1) and cap-style at $11.50-14.90 (MOQ 10). No Israeli retail comp exists at all: Zap/ACE/KSP return only steering-wheel locks (116-517 ILS); the 599 ILS comp is a locksmith-installed OBD 'safe' (Akeyless-type), not a bolt-on cap, so 199 ILS is untested. Patents exist (US app 20240413563 'OBD II Port Lock', US 11999230/12600320 adapter-with-notification, Zammitek 'patented' steel cover), so the IP level should be medium, not low; a generic screw-cap design is probably safe but must not copy a specific patented mechanism.

      Further verification required

      Real Israeli theft-vector demand but zero retail price anchor, vehicle-fit returns risk and patent density; order samples for the 5 best-selling Israeli models (Toyota/Hyundai/Kia/Mazda/Skoda) before committing.
