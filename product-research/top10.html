#1 · OBD-II port lock / blocker score 83 · High confidence

Overview

A machined aluminium cap with a keyed lock that covers the car's OBD-II diagnostic socket so a thief cannot plug in a key-programming tool. Passive, 150 g, no electronics.

Why now: Israeli police data (Lahav 433) and Pointer's 2023 analysis both name key-cloning through the OBD socket as the leading theft method, often with a cellular jammer; after two years of falling thefts, attempts spiked about 25% in early 2026 (bhol, Feb 2026). No Israeli retailer sells a bolt-on port lock: Zap, ACE and KSP return only steering-wheel locks at ₪116–517 and a locksmith-installed OBD 'safe' at ₪599.

Evidence of demand: Israel-native: Pointer 2023 and bhol 2026 theft-method reports, Lahav 433 spike, Midrag locksmiths recommending OBD-line blocking; international: Globe and Mail and CTV coverage of OBD locks as a theft counter-measure. No Amazon sales tracker exists for this niche, so volume is unproven.

Suppliers & competition

Alibaba showroom listings at $6.25–7.00 (MOQ 1) for an aluminium cap-style lock and $11.50–18 (MOQ 2–10) for cap/door-lock systems from a Guangzhou maker; AliExpress wholesale from $1.59. Model uses $7.00 FOB. All listings must be re-quoted; ask for anodised 6061 aluminium, a keyed-alike option and the fit list by connector orientation.

No direct Israeli competitor observed. Adjacent anchors: steering locks ₪116–517 (ACE sale ₪116), OBD tracking/immobiliser systems ₪599+ installed. Recommended ₪179 sits between the two with no brand to beat; the risk is that an AliExpress-IL seller undercuts at ₪40–60.

Quality concerns: Fit is vehicle-specific (port position and orientation differ by model, some ports are recessed); forum users note that a determined thief can probe the CAN wires behind the lock, so it is a deterrent, not an immobiliser; it blocks workshop diagnostics unless the owner keeps the key in the car. US utility/design patents exist (US app 20240413563, US 11999230), so the design must be a generic screw-cap, not a copy.
